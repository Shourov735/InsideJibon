Lesson 4.1

Chapter 4: Quadratic Equations

Welcome to Chapter 4. In this lesson, we will explore the fundamental properties of quadratic equations. Building upon your understanding of linear equations, quadratics introduce a new layer of complexity by involving variables raised to the second power.

A standard quadratic equation is written in the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, and c are known numbers, where a ≠ 0, and x is the unknown variable. The highest exponent of the variable is two, distinguishing it from linear forms.

lightbulb Key Concept

The graph of a univariate quadratic function is a parabola whose axis of symmetry is parallel to the y-axis. The roots of the quadratic equation represent the x-intercepts of the parabola.

By the end of this module, you will be proficient in solving these equations using multiple methods: factoring, completing the square, and applying the quadratic formula. We will start with visual representations to ground the abstract concepts in geometric reality.
